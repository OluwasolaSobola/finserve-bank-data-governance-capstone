# FinServe Bank – Current-State Data Governance Assessment

## Purpose

This assessment documents the initial Data Governance review conducted for FinServe Bank as part of the MBanking 2025 digital transformation and planned merger programme.

The assessment identified key governance, Data Quality, regulatory and accountability challenges requiring a structured enterprise Data Governance programme.

## Organisation Context

FinServe Bank is the banking case-study environment used for this capstone.

The organisation operates:

- 85 branches
- Approximately 2,800 employees
- Approximately $12 billion in assets
- Operations across five states

The governance programme was developed in the context of:

- MBanking 2025 digital transformation
- Planned acquisition of MidWest Community Bank
- Increasing regulatory requirements
- Data Quality concerns
- Growing use of AI and analytics

## Key Governance Findings

The current-state assessment identified:

- 18 regulatory findings
- 6 high-risk regulatory findings
- 35% duplicate customer records
- 28% incomplete customer records
- Lack of a formal enterprise Data Governance framework
- Unclear data ownership and accountability
- Inconsistent Data Quality controls
- Inadequate protection of sensitive customer information
- Limited governance and oversight of AI models

## Key Governance Gaps

### Governance Framework

A formal enterprise-wide Data Governance framework was not established, resulting in inconsistent governance practices across business areas.

### Data Ownership & Accountability

Responsibilities for important data assets were not consistently defined, creating uncertainty around ownership, Data Quality and issue resolution.

### Data Quality

Significant duplicate and incomplete customer records created operational, regulatory and decision-making risks.

### Privacy & Sensitive Data

Sensitive customer information required stronger classification, access and protection controls.

### Metadata Management

Business definitions, ownership information, metadata and data relationships required greater standardisation and governance.

### AI Governance

AI models required formal governance covering accountability, risk classification, documentation, validation, fairness, privacy and ongoing monitoring.

## Governance Priorities

The assessment established the following priorities:

1. Establish an enterprise Data Governance framework
2. Define Data Owners and Data Stewards
3. Improve Data Quality and monitoring
4. Identify and govern Critical Data Elements
5. Strengthen data classification and sensitive-data protection
6. Establish metadata management standards
7. Introduce formal AI Governance and Responsible AI controls
8. Establish governance issue-management and escalation processes
9. Develop governance KPIs and reporting
10. Support MBanking 2025 and merger data integration

## Governance Response

The findings informed the development of:

- Data Governance Charter
- Federated Data Governance operating model
- Stakeholder and accountability model
- Critical Data Inventory
- Governance policies and standards
- Data Quality Framework
- Metadata and catalogue approach
- Data classification and PII controls
- AI Governance Framework
- Issue-management process
- Governance monitoring and KPIs
- 12-month implementation roadmap
